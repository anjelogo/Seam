## Rubric (verbatim)

### Try-on render review

You are judging untextured cloth-simulation renders of real second-hand garments on a grey mannequin,
against the listing photo of each garment. Judge geometry only: prints, colours, buttons and zips are not
rendered, so ignore them.

Each sheet has two rows. Pose A (top row) is the simulation pose, arms about 45 degrees from vertical.
Pose B (bottom row) is the web pose, arms about 20 degrees from vertical. Tiles, left to right: front,
back, side, three-quarter, a top-down shoulder view, then three "cap" tiles (front, back, top-down) that
paint the mannequin's shoulder caps green where cloth lies within 2 cm of the skin. The cloth is drawn
in one flat teal colour.

For every sheet, answer each question for pose A and for pose B separately, Yes or No, each with a
one-line reason that points at what you see:

1. Fabric covers both shoulders: cloth runs continuously over the top of both shoulders from the
   neckline to the sleeves, with no bare shoulder.
2. The sleeves sit at the shoulder: the sleeve/body junction is at the shoulder point and the sleeves
   rest on the upper arms (not slid down the arm, not standing off the arm like a cape).
3. The neckline matches the listing: for a crew neck, the crew sits at the base of the neck and reads
   round, not a V, a notch, a deep scoop or a boat neck; for any other collar, it reads like the
   listing's.
4. No strap, halter or cold-shoulder geometry: nothing at the shoulder is cut away or reduced to a strap.
5. Left and right are symmetric: neckline, sleeves and drape mirror each other.
6. It is plausible as worn: it hangs on the body like a real garment, with no clipping through the body
   and no floating cloth.

A sheet PASSES only if all six answers are Yes in both poses. End with a summary table: sheet, verdict
(PASS, or FAIL with the failing question numbers and poses).
